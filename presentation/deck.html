01Problem Statement

Two stations, one narrow satellite link, and no unified picture

What makes this hard

  • Stations sit ~12,000 km from India, reachable only by satellite link with limited, intermittent bandwidth.
  • Resupply happens once or twice a year by ship. A decision missed in March cannot be corrected in June.
  • Infrastructure, power, logistics and weather data are logged by separate systems and reported separately.
  • Nobody sees one screen that says "this station is in trouble, and here is why."
The cost of getting it wrong: Dakshin Gangotri, India's first station (1983), was decommissioned in 1990 after being buried under accumulating ice. Remote assets degrade quietly when their condition is not tracked continuously.

02Proposed Solution

A live software mirror of each station, split into four pillars

Environment

Temperature, wind chill, sustained wind and gusts, visibility, pressure, snow depth.

Energy

Site demand, generator load, diesel reserve, solar and wind contribution, battery bank.

Infrastructure

Habitat temperature, HVAC health, water and waste tanks, structural integrity, uplink latency.

Logistics

Crew on station, rations endurance, next resupply window, medical kits, cargo awaiting.

How it addresses the problem

  • One pane, not four tools. Both stations, all four domains, one screen, updating live.
  • Ranked alerts, not raw numbers. The system says what is wrong and how badly, critical first.
  • Cross-domain reasoning. Rations endurance is compared against the resupply window — the kind of link a single-domain dashboard cannot make.

What makes it different

  • Thresholds are auditable code, not dashboard settings. Every rule lives in one tested module NCPOR can review and tune.
  • Built for a bad link. The server renders HTML and ships small JSON — no heavy bundle over satellite.
  • Zero-setup evaluation. Two commands and it runs, database included. No install ceremony for reviewers.

Methodology
  • All physics and alert thresholds sit in one I/O-free module, so the API and the dashboard derive status from the same source and cannot disagree.
  • That purity is what makes the rules testable without a database — 14 automated tests, including a 1000-tick invariant sweep.
Modelling
  • Wind chill via the Environment Canada / NWS index — the standard used for exposure warnings.
  • Austral seasonal temperature curve plus diurnal variation and a bounded random walk with mean reversion.
  • Alert severity in two tiers: critical warning

04Prototype · Working Demo

Not a mockup — a running system, screenshotted live

Maitri · blizzard in progress Wind chill −57.4 °C, sustained 53 kts, visibility 41 m. Three criticals raised: outdoor operations suspended, blizzard, whiteout.
Bharati · supply shortfall Rations last 47 days, next resupply in 62. The system flags the 15-day gap as critical — a cross-domain link no single dashboard would catch.
Working today: full backend, 5 REST endpoints with input validation, database layer, alert engine, live dashboard refreshing every 5 seconds, 14 passing tests.
Honest scope: telemetry is simulated by a physics model, not a live NCPOR feed. The ingestion layer is built and typed — connecting a real feed replaces the simulator, not the system.

05Feasibility & Viability

Ordinary web technology aimed at an extraordinary environment

Why it is feasible: no exotic hardware and no new sensors are required. NCPOR already collects this data; the gap is that it is scattered. The whole system runs on a standard laptop or an existing station server, and the entire stack is open source.

ChallengeHow it is handled
Limited satellite bandwidth Server renders HTML; the browser polls a small JSON payload and patches numbers in place instead of reloading the page.
Connectivity blackouts Station side buffers readings and forwards them when the link returns. The console shows last-known state with an explicit timestamp, never a stale number pretending to be current.
Sensor failure or bad data Validation at ingestion with range checks per field; readings outside physical bounds are rejected and flagged rather than silently averaged in.
Thresholds differ per station and season Every threshold is a named constant in one reviewed module, so NCPOR can tune rules without touching the UI.
Data access and authorisation Phased rollout — begin with non-sensitive operational telemetry, add role-based access before any control capability is considered.

06Impact & Benefits

Earlier warning, on the decisions that cannot be undone

Crew safety

−57 °C

Wind chill and habitat temperature breaches surface as criticals while there is still time to suspend traverses.

Logistics lead time

15 days

The rations-versus-resupply gap is flagged months before the shortfall, when the ship schedule can still change.

Energy

4 sources

Diesel, solar, wind and battery tracked together, so refuelling is scheduled on a trend rather than on a low-level alarm.

Who benefits

  • Station crew — hazards escalate before they become emergencies, during months when no help can arrive.
  • NCPOR operations, Goa — one authoritative picture of both stations instead of reconciling separate reports.
  • MoES planners — resupply and fuel decisions backed by trends rather than by the last message received.

Wider value

  • Economic — a missed resupply window costs an entire season; fuel is the single largest logistics expense of a polar station.
  • Environmental — visible generator load and renewable contribution support diesel reduction, in line with Antarctic Treaty environmental obligations.
  • Scalable — the station profile is data, not code. Additional stations, Himalayan high-altitude posts or offshore platforms are a configuration entry.
